01 / PROJECT

RSIDEA

2026 – Present

A remote sensing agent for landslide monitoring and analysis

RSIDEA explores how remote sensing and tool-using AI agents can support the full landslide disaster cycle: pre-event risk analysis, event-time impact assessment, and post-event recovery planning.

The research plan integrates multi-temporal imagery, elevation and terrain factors, rainfall, historical landslide records, and exposed roads and settlements. LLMs and vision-language models interpret tasks and orchestrate specialist models and GIS tools.

Research plan · June 2026 – March 2028

01 / BEFORE
Risk analysis

Study landslide susceptibility and rainfall scenarios using terrain, historical events, and precipitation data.

02 / DURING
Detection & impact

Explore change detection and landslide segmentation, then map potentially affected roads and settlements with GIS.

03 / AFTER
Recovery support

Investigate multi-factor screening of reconstruction candidates and monitor surface change and vegetation recovery.

Agent workflow

Task interpretation → Data retrieval → Planning → Tool execution → Validation → Evidence-linked reporting

Validation & traceability

Planned experiments compare single- and multi-temporal models, terrain fusion, and fixed versus agent-orchestrated workflows on independent regions or events. Spatial metrics come from specialist tools; data sources, parameters, model versions, and execution logs are retained for verification.

Expected outputs: an agent prototype, a multi-source case library, reusable analysis tools, and maps and reports linked to their underlying evidence.

Project lead · Overall research design, landslide recognition algorithms, experimental evaluation, and progress coordination.

Supervised by Assoc. Prof. Ailong Ma · State Key Laboratory of Information Engineering in Surveying, Mapping and Remote Sensing, Wuhan University.
